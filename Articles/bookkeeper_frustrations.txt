Title: Frustrated With Your Bookkeeper - 8 Common Problems and How to Fix Them
Author: Matthew Jacob
Date: November 13, 2026
ReadTime: 7 min
Image: bookkeeper_frustrations.jpg


Summary:
Unanswered emails, books that are months behind, surprise bills and penalty notices from the IRS. If any of that sounds familiar, you're not alone. Small business owners vent about the same bookkeeping frustrations again and again. Here are the eight most common problems, what's usually causing them, and what good bookkeeping should look like instead.

Section:1. "I can never get a straight answer"
The most common complaint about bookkeepers isn't the numbers. It's communication. Emails go unanswered for a week, questions arrive one at a time with no context, and when you ask what a report means you get jargon instead of an answer.
What good looks like: a predictable rhythm. You should know when your books will be done each month, get your open questions in one short list instead of a stream of emails, and be able to get on a quick call when you want to talk through your numbers.

Section:2. "My books are always months behind"
Many owners only find out their books are behind when the CPA asks for year-end reports, or when a lender asks for current financials and there aren't any.
What good looks like: a monthly close. Every bank, credit card and loan account reconciled to its statement each month, and financial statements in your inbox shortly after month-end. If your books are already behind, a one-time catch-up gets you to a clean starting point first.

Section:3. "The bill is different every month"
Hourly billing makes bookkeeping hard to budget, and it can feel like you're paying for your bookkeeper's learning curve. Owners hesitate to ask questions because every email might cost them.
What good looks like: a set monthly price based on how many accounts and transactions you have, agreed up front. Questions shouldn't come with a meter running.

Section:4. "I'm still doing half the work myself"
If you're still copying transactions from your bank, hunting receipts in your email and guessing at categories, you're doing bookkeeping, not running your business.
What good looks like: bank feeds connected to QuickBooks Online, bank rules that categorize repeat transactions automatically, and receipts captured as they happen. Your job should shrink to answering a few questions a month.

Section:5. "I got a penalty notice because of a mistake"
Late or wrong payroll filings, missed 1099s and sales tax returns that don't match your books lead to IRS and Arizona Department of Revenue notices. In Arizona, transaction privilege tax (TPT) is a common trouble spot, especially for contractors and short-term rental owners, because the rules depend on the type of revenue.
What good looks like: books that tie out. The sales tax you collected should match what you filed, payroll in your books should match your payroll reports, and contractor payments should be tracked all year so 1099s are easy in January.

Section:6. "I get reports, but I don't know what they mean"
A Profit and Loss and Balance Sheet are only useful if someone helps you read them. Many owners receive reports every month and never open them, because nobody explains what changed or why it matters.
What good looks like: a short review of your numbers each month. Where did margins slip? Which costs are creeping up? Are you charging enough? That kind of insight, like a light fractional CFO, is where bookkeeping actually pays for itself.

Section:7. "My last bookkeeper left me a mess"
Owners who switch bookkeepers often find duplicate transactions, unreconciled accounts, wrong opening balances or an entire year that was never entered. Sometimes the previous bookkeeper simply disappeared.
What good looks like: an honest assessment of where your books stand, a written plan to fix them, and a fixed quote for the cleanup before any work starts.

Section:8. "I don't know if my books are even right"
Trust is the root of every other frustration. If you don't believe your numbers, you can't use them to price jobs, hire, borrow or plan for taxes.
What good looks like: reconciliations you can see, reports that match your bank, and a bookkeeper who explains what they did and stands behind it.

Section:How to Switch Bookkeepers Without Losing a Month
1.  Get admin access to your own QuickBooks Online file if you don't already have it. The file belongs to you, not your bookkeeper.
2.  Download recent bank and credit card statements so the new bookkeeper can check the last reconciliation.
3.  Ask your current bookkeeper for open items: unreconciled transactions, pending payroll filings and anything due soon.
4.  Have the new bookkeeper review the file before you end the old relationship, so nothing falls through the cracks.
5.  Set a start month, and let the new bookkeeper catch up anything older as a one-time project.

Final: You shouldn't have to chase your bookkeeper, wonder if your numbers are right, or get surprised by a penalty notice. Good bookkeeping is predictable, accurate and explained in plain English.
