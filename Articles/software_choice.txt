Title: QuickBooks Online vs. Xero vs. Spreadsheets - Choosing Bookkeeping Software for a Small Business
Author: Matthew Jacob
Date: March 24, 2026
ReadTime: 7 min
Image: software_choice.jpg


Summary:
"What accounting software should I use?" might be the most asked bookkeeping question online. The honest answer: the best software is the one your bookkeeper and CPA know well and that fits how your business works. Here's how QuickBooks Online, Xero and spreadsheets compare for small businesses, and how to decide.

Section:Spreadsheets: Fine to Start, Hard to Grow
A spreadsheet works for a brand-new side business with a handful of transactions. It's free and flexible. But there are no bank feeds, no reconciliation tools, no automatic reports and plenty of room for formula errors. Once you have more than a few dozen transactions a month, or you need statements for a lender, it's time to move on.

Section:QuickBooks Online
The most widely used small business accounting software in the US. Strengths: almost every US bookkeeper and CPA knows it, it connects to most banks and apps, and it handles invoicing, bills, payroll add-ons, job and project tracking, and sales tax. Downsides: price increases over the years, and the higher tiers can get expensive. Plans differ in users and features, so pick the level you need, not the most expensive one. Our <a href="/Articles/Article_HTMLs/How_to_Set_Up_QuickBooks_Online_for_Your_Business">QuickBooks Online setup guide</a> walks through getting started.

Section:Xero
A strong alternative that's popular internationally. Strengths: clean interface, unlimited users on most plans and good bank reconciliation. Downsides: fewer US bookkeepers and CPAs use it, and some US-specific features like payroll rely on add-ons. It's a good choice if your accountant prefers it.

Section:Other Options
Free tools like Wave can work for very small businesses. Industry tools like construction or property management software often still need a general ledger like QuickBooks behind them.

Section:How to Choose
1.  Ask your CPA and bookkeeper what they use. Software they know saves you money.
2.  List the apps you rely on, like your payment processor, payroll provider or e-commerce platform, and check that they connect.
3.  Think about growth. Will you need inventory, multiple entities, classes or locations?
4.  Count users. Some plans limit how many people can log in.
5.  Avoid switching often. Moving software mid-year creates cleanup work.

Section:Desktop Users Have a Decision to Make
If you're still on QuickBooks Desktop, Intuit has been steering users to the online version and retiring older Desktop releases.
