Year-End Payroll Checklist for Arizona Employers - What to Do Before W-2s Are Due

Matthew Jacob

November 23, 2026 - 7 min read

A year-end payroll checklist keeps Arizona employers ready for W-2s, the A1-R and federal returns. All are due by February 1, 2027, and 2026 W-2s add new boxes for tips and overtime. Here's what's due, what changed this year, and what to clean up in December so January goes smoothly.

Why a Year-End Payroll Checklist Matters This Year

If you have even one employee, January is a busy month. Your W-2s, your federal payroll returns and your Arizona withholding reconciliation all land within days of each other. Most of that work depends on payroll records you can still fix in December, but not after the forms go out.

This year there's more to get right. The 2026 Form W-2 has new codes for tips and overtime under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and the IRS split box 14 into two parts. A year-end payroll checklist is the easiest way to catch problems while they're still cheap to fix.

If you pay contractors instead of, or alongside, employees, 1099s run on a separate track. See getting ready for 1099 season for that side of January.

Key Year-End Payroll Deadlines for 2026 Wages

January 31, 2027 falls on a Sunday, so most of these deadlines move to the next business day.

1. Form W-2 to employees: by February 1, 2027.

2. Forms W-2 and W-3 to the Social Security Administration: by February 1, 2027, whether you file on paper or electronically.

3. Arizona Form A1-R, the annual withholding reconciliation, with copies of your W-2s: due January 31, which moves to February 1, 2027 under Arizona's weekend rule.

4. Arizona Form A1-QRT for the fourth quarter: due the same day. Employers who made every payment on time for the quarter get until February 10.

5. Federal Form 941 for the fourth quarter and the annual Form 940 for federal unemployment: generally due at the end of January, which also moves to February 1, 2027. Form 940 can be filed by February 10 if all of your federal unemployment tax was deposited on time.

Arizona now requires W-2s to be submitted to the Department of Revenue electronically, and the IRS requires e-filing if you file 10 or more information returns in total, counting W-2s and 1099s together. Most payroll software handles both for you, but confirm it's turned on.

What's New on the 2026 Form W-2

Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, employees may be able to deduct some qualified tips and overtime on their own tax returns. To support that, the 2026 Form W-2 adds new reporting:

1. Box 12, code TP: the total cash tips the employee reported to you.

2. Box 12, code TT: the total qualified overtime compensation.

3. Box 12, code TA: employer contributions to a Trump account, if you offer that program.

4. Box 14 is now split into 14a for other information and 14b for the Treasury tipped occupation code.

If you run a restaurant, salon or any business where employees receive tips, or you pay overtime, check with your payroll provider now that these amounts are being tracked separately. Rebuilding a year of tip and overtime detail in January is painful. Your employees will be looking for these numbers when they file.

Your December Payroll Cleanup List

1. Confirm every employee's legal name, Social Security number and mailing address match their Form W-4 and Social Security card. Name and number mismatches are the most common W-2 problem.

2. Ask employees who moved this year to update their address before the W-2s print.

3. Compare year-to-date payroll totals to your books. Gross wages and payroll tax expense on your Profit and Loss should tie to your payroll reports. If they don't, find out why now.

4. Review taxable fringe benefits that need to be on the W-2, such as personal use of a company vehicle or S-corporation owner health insurance. Your tax professional can tell you which apply.

5. Reconcile your federal and Arizona tax deposits to the liabilities your payroll reports show, quarter by quarter.

6. Make sure every worker you paid as an employee got a paycheck through payroll, and every contractor was paid outside of it. If you're not sure how someone should be classified, read employee vs. contractor.

7. Schedule your last payroll of the year carefully. Wages count in the year they're paid, not the year they're earned, so a paycheck dated January 2 belongs on next year's W-2.

Arizona Withholding and the A-4

Arizona withholding is based on Form A-4, which each employee completes to choose a withholding percentage. If an employee never turned one in, Arizona's default rate applies. January is a good time to remind employees they can submit a new A-4 if they want to change their withholding for the coming year. A blank Arizona A-4, along with the W-4 and I-9, is on our free resources page.

The A1-R pulls together everything you withheld for Arizona during the year, so it only balances if your quarterly A1-QRT returns and deposits were right. If a quarter was filed late or with the wrong amount, fix it before the annual return goes in. Arizona also requires electronic payments once your withholding liability reaches $500 or more for the year.

Don't forget Arizona unemployment insurance. The fourth quarter report goes to the Arizona Department of Economic Security, not the Department of Revenue, and is also due at the end of January.

Year-End Payroll in QuickBooks Online

If you run payroll through QuickBooks Online Payroll, Intuit can file and pay your federal and Arizona payroll taxes and file W-2s for you, but only if your company and employee setup is correct. Before year-end:

1. Open each employee's profile and check their name, address, Social Security number, and W-4 and A-4 details.

2. Review your payroll tax settings and confirm your Arizona withholding account number and unemployment rate are entered and current.

3. Run a payroll summary report for the year and compare it to your Profit and Loss and your bank account.

4. Record any off-cycle checks, bonuses or manual payroll adjustments before your final payroll of the year.

If you pay yourself through payroll as an S-corporation owner, this is also the time to confirm your salary for the year is where you and your tax professional planned it. For background, see owner draws vs. payroll.

What Happens If You Miss a Payroll Deadline

Late or incorrect W-2s can bring IRS penalties per form, and the amount goes up the longer they go uncorrected. Arizona charges its own penalties and interest for late withholding returns and payments. Errors also create work: corrected W-2c forms, amended quarterly returns and unhappy employees waiting on tax documents. If a notice has already arrived, read what to do after an IRS or Arizona penalty notice.
