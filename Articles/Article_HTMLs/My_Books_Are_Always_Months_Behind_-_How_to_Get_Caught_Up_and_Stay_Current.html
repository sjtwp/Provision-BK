My Books Are Always Months Behind - How to Get Caught Up and Stay Current

Matthew Jacob

September 11, 2026 - 7 min read

You usually find out your books are behind at the worst time: your CPA needs year-end numbers, a lender wants current financials, or you can't tell whether last quarter was profitable. Behind books aren't just an inconvenience. They cost you money in rushed tax prep, missed deductions and decisions made blind. Here's how books fall behind, what catching up actually involves, and how to stay current for good.

How Books Fall Behind

It rarely happens all at once. A busy season pushes bookkeeping to next month. A bank feed disconnects and nobody notices. A bookkeeper takes on too many clients, or the owner is doing it themselves at night and simply runs out of nights. Three months becomes six, and six becomes a year.

What Behind Books Really Cost You

1. Higher tax prep fees. Many CPAs charge extra to work from books that aren't reconciled, or send them back.

2. Missed deductions. Receipts you can't find in April are deductions you don't take.

3. Late filings. If you can't see your numbers, it's easy to miss payroll, sales tax or estimated payments.

4. Lost financing. Banks and SBA lenders want current financial statements, usually within the last 60 to 90 days.

5. Bad decisions. Pricing, hiring and spending based on your bank balance instead of real profit.

What Catch-Up Bookkeeping Involves

Catching up isn't just typing in old transactions. A proper catch-up usually follows these steps:

Gather statements. Every bank, credit card and loan statement for the missing months.

Connect or re-connect bank feeds in QuickBooks Online and import the missing history.

Categorize transactions, starting with rules for recurring vendors so the volume goes faster.

Reconcile each account, month by month, to the ending balance on each statement.

Fix what's broken along the way, like duplicates, transfers recorded as income or personal expenses mixed in.

Produce financial statements for each month or quarter so you can see the trend, not just the year-end total.

Most small businesses can be caught up in a few weeks, depending on how many months and accounts are involved. Learn more about our catch-up and cleanup service.

How to Stay Current After You Catch Up

The fix for behind books is a monthly close, a repeatable routine done on the same schedule every month:

1. Bank feeds reviewed weekly or at least monthly.

2. Receipts captured as they happen, not in a shoebox.

3. Every account reconciled once the statement arrives.

4. Financial statements delivered shortly after month-end.

5. A short list of open questions answered by the owner each month.

Our free Monthly Bookkeeping Close Checklist walks through each step if you want to see what a close looks like.

Doing It Yourself vs. Hiring Help

If you have one or two accounts and a few dozen transactions a month, you can stay current yourself with an hour a week. Once you add payroll, sales tax or several accounts, or once you've fallen behind more than once, it's usually cheaper to hand it off than to keep losing weekends.
