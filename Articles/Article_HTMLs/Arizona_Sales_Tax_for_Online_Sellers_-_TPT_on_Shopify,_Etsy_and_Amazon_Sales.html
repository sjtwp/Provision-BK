Arizona Sales Tax for Online Sellers - TPT on Shopify, Etsy and Amazon Sales

Matthew Jacob

July 7, 2026 - 7 min read

Selling online from Arizona raises a lot of sales tax questions. Do Etsy and Amazon collect it for you? What about sales on your own Shopify site? Do you need a license? Arizona's version of sales tax, the transaction privilege tax or TPT, has its own rules. Here's an overview for Arizona-based online sellers and how to keep your books in line with your returns.

What TPT Is

Arizona doesn't technically have a sales tax. It has a transaction privilege tax, a tax on the privilege of doing business in the state, which most businesses pass on to customers like a sales tax. Retail sales are generally taxable. Many cities have their own TPT rates on top of the state and county rates, and the Arizona Department of Revenue administers most of them.

Marketplace Sales: Etsy, Amazon and Similar

Arizona has marketplace facilitator rules. Large marketplaces like Amazon, Etsy and eBay generally collect and remit Arizona TPT on sales made through their platforms. That means you usually aren't collecting tax on those sales yourself, but you should still track them, and you may still need a TPT license and to report them, depending on your situation. Check with ADOR or your tax preparer.

Your Own Website: Shopify, Squarespace and Others

Sales through your own site are different. If you're based in Arizona and sell taxable products to Arizona customers, you're generally responsible for collecting and remitting TPT yourself. Shopify and similar platforms can calculate tax at checkout, but only if you set it up. Out-of-state sales may be subject to other states' rules once you pass their economic thresholds.

Licensing and Filing

Arizona businesses making taxable sales generally need a TPT license from ADOR. Returns are filed monthly, quarterly or annually depending on your tax liability, and are typically due the month after the reporting period. Filing late brings penalties and interest, so put the dates on a calendar.

Keeping Your Books in Line With Your Returns

1. Record sales at gross, with sales tax collected in a liability account, not income.

2. Separate marketplace sales from your own website sales.

3. Record platform fees as expenses. See how to record processing fees.

4. Reconcile the tax you collected to what you filed every period.
