My Bookkeeping Bill Is Different Every Month - Hourly vs. Flat-Rate Bookkeeping

Matthew Jacob

September 18, 2026 - 6 min read

One month your bookkeeping invoice is $300, the next it's $700, and you're not sure why. Unpredictable bills are one of the fastest ways to lose trust in a bookkeeper. Usually the cause is hourly billing, not dishonesty. Here's how hourly and flat-rate bookkeeping compare, what drives the swings, and how to get a price you can actually budget for.

Why Hourly Bookkeeping Bills Swing

With hourly billing, anything that takes time changes your bill: a busy sales month, a bank feed that broke and had to be fixed, a few extra questions, or a bookkeeper who is still learning your business. You see the total, but rarely the reasons.

Hourly vs. Flat-Rate: An Honest Comparison

Hourly billing. Pros: you only pay for time spent, and it can be cheap in quiet months. Cons: hard to budget, rewards slow work, and discourages you from asking questions.

Flat monthly rate. Pros: one known price every month, questions are included, and the bookkeeper is rewarded for being efficient. Cons: you may pay the same in a slow month, and the price is reviewed if your business grows a lot.

Per-transaction or tiered pricing sits in between: a base price up to a certain volume, then more for each block above it. It works if your volume is steady.

What a Fair Flat Rate Is Based On

A flat price shouldn't be a guess. It should come from a few things you can see:

1. How many bank, credit card and loan accounts are reconciled each month.

2. Your typical monthly transaction count.

3. Extras like payroll, sales tax returns, inventory or job costing.

4. Whether your books start out current.

If those change a lot, the price should be revisited, with a conversation first, not a surprise invoice.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

1. What exactly is included in the monthly price?

2. Are emails and calls included, or billed separately?

3. What would cause my price to change, and how will I be told?

4. Is catch-up work quoted separately and up front?

5. Is there a guarantee if I'm not happy?

Is Your Current Bill Too High

A higher bill isn't always a worse deal. If you're paying more but getting reconciled books, statements on time and real answers, that may be money well spent.
