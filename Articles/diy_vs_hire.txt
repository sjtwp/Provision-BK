Title: DIY Bookkeeping vs. Hiring a Bookkeeper - When a Side Business Needs Help
Author: Matthew Jacob
Date: July 28, 2026
ReadTime: 6 min
Image: diy_vs_hire.jpg


Summary:
A common question online goes like this: "My business made $5,000 last year. Do I need a bookkeeper?" Often the honest answer is no, not yet. Small side businesses can usually handle their own books with the right setup. Here's how to do it yourself well, and the signs that it's time to hand it off.

Section:When DIY Bookkeeping Makes Sense
1.  One bank account and maybe one card.
2.  A few dozen transactions a month.
3.  No employees and no sales tax.
4.  You're comfortable spending 30 to 60 minutes a month on it.
If that's you, save your money and do it yourself.

Section:A Simple DIY Setup That Works
Open a separate business checking account and card. See <a href="/Articles/Article_HTMLs/Mixing_Personal_and_Business_Expenses_-_Why_You_Need_a_Separate_Business_Bank_Account">why you need a separate business account</a>.
Use simple software with bank feeds, like QuickBooks Online, instead of a spreadsheet once activity picks up.
Capture receipts as you go with your phone.
Once a month, categorize transactions and reconcile your account to the statement.
Set aside money for taxes from every payment. See <a href="/Articles/Article_HTMLs/How_Much_to_Set_Aside_for_Taxes_-_Estimated_Tax_Payments_for_Arizona_Small_Business_Owners">how much to set aside for taxes</a>.

Section:Signs It's Time to Get Help
1.  You're more than two months behind, more than once.
2.  You've added employees, contractors or sales tax.
3.  You spend more than a few hours a month on bookkeeping.
4.  Your CPA charges you extra to fix the books at tax time.
5.  You need financial statements for a lender, partner or investor.
6.  You're making decisions without knowing your real profit.

Section:The Middle Ground
You don't have to choose all or nothing. Options include a one-time setup so your DIY books start right, a quarterly review where a bookkeeper checks your work, or a year-end cleanup before taxes.
