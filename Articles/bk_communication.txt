Title: I Can Never Get a Straight Answer From My Bookkeeper - How to Fix Bookkeeper Communication
Author: Matthew Jacob
Date: September 4, 2026
ReadTime: 6 min
Image: bk_communication.jpg


Summary:
You email a simple question and hear back a week later, if at all. When you do get an answer, it's full of jargon. Poor communication is the number one complaint owners have about their bookkeepers, and it's usually a process problem, not a people problem. Here's why it happens, what a healthy back-and-forth looks like, and how to fix it with your current bookkeeper or a new one.

Section:Why Bookkeepers Go Quiet
Most slow replies come from the same few causes:
1.  Too many clients. A solo bookkeeper juggling 60 or more accounts can't answer everyone the same day.
2.  No set schedule. If your books get done whenever there's time, so do your emails.
3.  Questions arrive one at a time. Ten separate emails about ten transactions turn into ten small interruptions, and they pile up.
4.  Hourly billing. Some bookkeepers avoid long replies because they feel awkward billing for them, and some owners avoid asking because they don't want the bill.
5.  Unclear ownership. Nobody agreed on who answers what, or how fast.

Section:What Good Bookkeeper Communication Looks Like
You don't need a reply in five minutes. You need to know what to expect. A healthy setup usually has:
A set close date. You know your books will be done by a certain day each month.
One list of open questions. Instead of a stream of emails, you get one short list of uncategorized or unclear transactions, ideally with a guess already filled in so you just confirm.
A stated response time. For example, replies within one business day for normal questions, and same day for anything urgent like payroll.
A standing review call. Fifteen or thirty minutes each month, or each quarter, to walk through your numbers.
Plain English. If you ask what your Balance Sheet means, you should get an answer you can repeat to your spouse or your banker.

Section:Questions You Should Always Be Able to Get Answered
If your bookkeeper can't answer these quickly, something is off:
1.  Are all of my accounts reconciled, and through what month?
2.  How much did I make last month, and how does that compare to the same month last year?
3.  What do I owe in sales tax, payroll tax or estimated tax right now?
4.  Which expenses went up the most this quarter?
5.  Is there anything in my books that you're unsure about?

Section:How to Fix It With Your Current Bookkeeper
Before you switch, try resetting expectations. Send one email that asks for three things: a close date each month, one consolidated list of questions, and a response time you can both live with. Offer a short monthly call. Many bookkeepers welcome the structure because it makes their work easier too.
Give it two or three months. If the close date keeps slipping and emails still disappear, the problem isn't the process.

Section:When It's Time to Move On
If you've asked clearly and nothing changes, it's reasonable to look for someone new. When you interview bookkeepers, ask how they communicate, not just what they charge: How often will I hear from you? How do you send questions? What happens if I need something urgently?
